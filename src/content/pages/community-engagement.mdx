## Observations

Effective community consultation is essential for major local projects to
gain widespread support and earn the trust of the people they affect. To
be effective, it is vital to genuinely involve the community wherever
possible in the design and delivery of a project.

Poor or absent engagement allows misinformation and opposition to gain
momentum, which can delay or derail a project through planning objections
and lengthy disputes. Project teams that invest early and consistently in
engagement generally see stronger community support and fewer disputes
than those that treat engagement as an afterthought.

### Community committees

Establishing an independent community consultative committee, maintained
for the life of a project, gives residents a direct channel to raise
issues and receive factual, timely information. Committees work best when
they have an independent chair and a membership that reflects the
community they represent.

### Communications

Clear, up-to-date project information matters. At a minimum, a project
should publish:

- who is responsible for the project and how to contact them
- current project status, milestones and any planning conditions
- how to make a complaint, and what happens next
- links to relevant project documents

### Coordination

Where several projects affect the same area, project teams should
coordinate with each other and with the local council to minimise
cumulative impacts, such as overlapping construction schedules or
competing demand on local services and accommodation.

## Recommendations

- Start engagement early, well before formal approval processes begin.
- Identify and build relationships with community stakeholders, including
  those who oppose the project.
- Establish a community consultative committee with an appropriate charter
  and independent chair.
- Provide accessible information channels, including in-person sessions,
  an easily found project website, and a way to ask questions
  anonymously.
- Set up a transparent complaints process, with a public register of
  complaints and outcomes (with personal details masked for privacy).
- Communicate construction and transport plans clearly, and restore any
  affected infrastructure to the same or better condition.
- Consider a community benefit fund, governed with genuine community
  input, that prioritises residents most directly affected.
